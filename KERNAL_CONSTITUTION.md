# The KERNAL Constitution

*The supreme law of the KERNAL platform. Every contributor, maintainer, and AI agent must follow these articles.*

---

## Article 1 — Accuracy over quantity
One correct, verified lesson is worth more than ten rushed ones. Never publish unverified content. Every claim must be traceable to an authoritative source.

## Article 2 — Understanding over memorization
Every concept must explain WHY it exists before explaining HOW it works. A learner who understands the problem never needs to memorize the solution.

## Article 3 — Practice over passive reading
Every topic must produce something. No topic ends with "read and next." Playgrounds, exercises, debug challenges, and projects are mandatory.

## Article 4 — Accessibility by default
WCAG 2.1 AA compliance is not optional. Semantic HTML, ARIA labels, keyboard navigation, focus indicators, color contrast, and reduced-motion support are required from the start.

## Article 5 — Privacy by default
No tracking. No analytics without consent. No accounts. No cloud sync. No data sent to any server. All progress is local and exportable. User data belongs to the user.

## Article 6 — No unnecessary backend dependency
KERNAL must run entirely client-side. No Node server. No database. No serverless functions. If a feature requires a server, it is not a KERNAL feature.

## Article 7 — Open and portable content
All content is stored in open JSON format. No content is locked to the engine. The same structured content must be readable by any future client — mobile app, CLI, browser extension, or API.

## Article 8 — Version-aware documentation
Every lesson knows which technology version it targets and when it was last verified. Outdated content must be flagged, updated, or archived — never silently left stale.

## Article 9 — No dead functionality
Every feature that ships must be used and maintained. Abandoned features must be removed. KERNAL has no graveyard of half-finished work.

## Article 10 — Progressive complexity
Beginners start simple. Advanced content is available on demand. The platform never overwhelms a new user with complexity they have not asked for.

## Article 11 — Source-verified technical information
Technical claims must cite authoritative sources: MDN, official language documentation, W3C or WHATWG specifications. AI-generated content must be verified before publication.

## Article 12 — User-controlled learning data
Users must be able to export, import, and delete all their data at any time. KERNAL never holds user data hostage. The data export must be human-readable JSON.

## Article 13 — The core must not depend on any content
The KERNAL engine never hard-codes lesson text, technology-specific behavior, or assumptions about what content exists. Adding a new technology = adding a new content folder. No engine changes required.

## Article 14 — Every topic must produce something
A learner does not finish a topic by reading. They finish it by building, debugging, or verifying. If a topic has no hands-on component, it is incomplete.

## Article 15 — Every concept must explain why it exists
Before syntax, before examples, before exercises: explain the problem that made this concept necessary. A developer who understands the WHY is a developer who can solve novel problems.
